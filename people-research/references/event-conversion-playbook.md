# Event conversion playbook

Seeded Sept 24, 2026, from the user's own transcripts and the Long Horizon Agents Hack prep.

## Thesis
A hackathon is an onsite interview: the organizers' product and engineering leaders are in the room as judges, and they're happy to give feedback on any screen. The demo is the interview; the feedback conversation is the door; the calendar hold is the conversion.

## Two modes + the tripwire
- **Build mode:** head down, demo working.
- **Conversion mode:** work the room — every remaining hour goes to people, not the repo.
- **Build-ready (the tripwire):** demo runs end-to-end, acceptance criteria met, video recordable. Not polished — *demonstrable*.
- The moment build-ready is true, switch modes. Polish has diminishing returns; a calendar hold with a judge compounds. (Builder holds the repo; the networker works the room.)

## The screen as pretext
Nobody at a hackathon refuses "can I get your feedback on our demo?" Every yes is a Qualify conversation. The two questions that open it:
- "What's the weakest claim in this?"
- "What would make this a yes for you?"

## The calendar-fill move
After any good feedback exchange: "This was genuinely useful — can we put 30 minutes on the calendar next week to go deeper?" Light activation, legitimate because they just praised your work. Three things on the calendar before leaving:
1. Interviews (hiring managers in the room).
2. Broker-target connections (the intro, scheduled).
3. Recruiter calls (the warm ones — "are you in touch with any recruiter recently?" finally asked live, to the right person).

## Broker stages (MECE)
Every broker is in exactly one stage at a time; Sustain loops back to Activate on the next ask.

**1. Scout** — no contact yet. Is this person a broker *for me*? (Asked to mutuals / answered by research, never to them.)
- Who did they last intro to a role?
- Do they reach my funnel companies — hiring managers, not just the logo?
- What's their currency — what do they care about, so the first touch isn't extractive?

**2. Qualify** — will they act, and where do they reach?
**3. Activate** — they said yes; make the intro happen and land.
- "What does the target care about right now?"
- "What should I send you that makes this easy to forward?"
- "Best channel — WhatsApp, iMessage, email?"
- "What's the objection they'd raise on me?"
**4. Sustain** — between asks and after outcomes; stay valuable so they broker again.
- "What is your priority for now?" (give before you ask)
- Report back: "Your intro to X led to Y." (brokers repeat what gets closed-looped)
- "Who are you trying to meet? Maybe I can return it."
- Debrief: "What worked / what didn't in how I showed up?"
- Reactivation, no guilt: "It's been a while — what's new on your side?"

## Qualify at the event vs beyond
The event runs Scout → Qualify-light only. Win condition: 3–5 qualified names + a reason to follow up — not intros.

**Qualify-light (live at the event):**
- "What is your priority for now?" — the universal opener.
- The feedback pair above — for 1-on-1s after the demo.
- Practitioner-judges only: "What's the promotion scene like on your team?" / "What are hiring managers actually looking for in AI-fluent PMs?"
- Earned-depth only: the scaled-experience objection ("larger companies say I don't have scaled-product experience — how do candidates approach this?").

**Qualify-deep (follow-up coffee chats):** now the direct questions come back — recruiter-in-touch, referral mapping ("who's best positioned to refer — recent switchers, founders, VCs, people who got offers elsewhere?"), funnel math ("4–5 company funnel aiming for 2–3 offers — where would you add or cut?"), career-path questions ("is that the right move at this point?", "do you see a path to X for me?").

**Cut at the event (too forward for first contact):** recruiter questions to strangers, referral mapping, funnel math, "do you know a good manager?" to non-practitioners. They skip Qualify and read as extraction.

## Build in public (offline + online)
Visibility during the event primes everything after it.

**Offline — on the hack floor:**
- Narrate the build as you go: visible whiteboard, demo screen facing out, talk about what you're building when people walk by.
- Run the villain→resolution demo on loop at your table — judges walking the floor see failure→fix without needing a formal pitch.
- Invite witnesses: "we're about to test the drift detector, want to watch?" Floor presence is passive Qualify — people self-select into your funnel.
- Tradeoff noted: ideas are visible to competitors, but at a 5-hour hackathon execution beats secrecy, and the seam is the moat.

**Online — in feeds and stories:**
- Post progress through the day: morning setup, midday tension, demo ready. Stories, not polished posts — raw beats produced during the event.
- Tag the event, sponsors, organizers — borrows their distribution and makes their post-win shoutout more likely (they've already been posting you).
- Get into *others'* stories: be visible, be quotable, keep the demo screen photogenic. Their audiences become your primed audience.
- The win reel then lands on people who've been watching all day — conversion, not cold announcement.
